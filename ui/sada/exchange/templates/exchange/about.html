Bringing the data back to the producer

The Producer Exchange is part of Fair Trade USA's Producer Intelligence Infrastructure. Producers describe a challenge in their own words and get back two things: what the data shows for their crop and region, and what peers have tried — grounded, cited, and theirs to weigh.

What Fair Trade means

Every product carrying the Fair Trade Certified™ label supports fair wages, safer working conditions, a healthier environment, and the chance for workers and farmers to invest in their communities and futures. The model links certification, consumer purchases, and community reinvestment in a cycle that strengthens communities and supply-chain transparency. The Community Development Fund (Premium)

For every Fair Trade Certified product sold, a business or importer pays a Fair Trade Premium into a worker-controlled Community Development Fund at origin. Producers decide together, democratically, how to invest it — in water infrastructure, healthcare, education, better farming, or local businesses. Paraphrased from the Fair Trade Certified FAQs. Enablement, not direction

FEX offers options and evidence. It never tells a producer what to do — the decision stays with the producer and their cooperative.

Every claim is cited

Insights and peer examples carry a source: the producer's own record, open data, a comparable Premium project, or a certification standard clause.

Consent and voice

Producers own their data and consent to sharing. Personal details are flagged for a human reviewer, never auto-erased, so authentic voice is preserved.

Light on resources

AI uses energy, so FEX uses it sparingly: small models where they are enough, no model training, and nothing running when no one is talking to it. Details

Multilingual

The exchange runs in the producer's language. Spanish is the confirmed focus language, with more available in this prototype.

How we keep FEX light

Fair Trade cares about the planet, and AI is not free for it: every answer uses electricity in a data center. We can't make that zero, so we designed FEX to use as little as it needs:

  • The right-sized model for each step. A small model (Claude Haiku) handles the questions, the read-back of your description and the summary. The larger model (Claude Sonnet) runs only for the cited answer.
  • No model training. FEX uses existing models on Amazon Bedrock. The Fair Trade standards were indexed once into a knowledge base, and facts are looked up rather than generated.
  • Nothing runs when no one is talking. FEX is serverless (Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, AWS Lambda, on-demand storage): no servers or GPUs sit idle waiting.
  • Short conversations, little data kept. A conversation closes after 2 hours without activity. Voice recordings are deleted once transcribed, attachments after a day, and only what you agree to save is kept.
  • For the climate, too. FEX exists to help producers adapt to drought, irregular rain and water stress, using open climate data.

Where the nature data comes from. FEX's water-stress figures come from WRI Aqueduct, which is listed in the TNFD Tools Catalogue (TNFD notes that inclusion does not imply endorsement). Rainfall comes from CHIRPS and prices from the World Bank, which are not in the catalogue; we plan to move to catalogue-listed sources where they fit.

What we can't claim yet. We haven't measured FEX's footprint, so we don't call it carbon-neutral. Amazon reports matching the electricity it uses with renewable energy; in production, Fair Trade USA could track FEX's estimated emissions with the AWS Customer Carbon Footprint Tool.
