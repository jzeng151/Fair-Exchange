# Vision Document: Fair Trade USA Producer Exchange Agent

## Executive Summary

The Producer Exchange Agent is a multilingual conversational agent, part of Fair Trade USA's Producer Intelligence Infrastructure, that enables farmers, fishers, and workers in certified producer organizations to describe a challenge they are facing (drought, falling prices, labor shortages, soil degradation) and receive two things back: what the data shows about that issue for their crop and region, and examples of what others have done in response. It addresses the gap in which producers generate valuable environmental, social, and economic evidence but rarely see climate, market, or regional data return to them in a usable form, while Fair Trade USA (FTUSA) only learns what producers were responding to secondhand, inferred from what they chose to fund. The expected outcome of the MVP is a working end-to-end exchange, built in a standalone AWS sandbox on synthetic data, in which one producer conversation produces grounded, standards-traceable guidance and is written back as a tagged, source-marked record joinable to the producer's existing Premium-use, survey, and certification data.

## Business Context

### Problem Statement

Producer organizations behind certified products generate valuable environmental, social, and economic information (climate observations, community investment outcomes, biodiversity metrics), but it sits fragmented across meeting minutes, voice notes, and photos in local systems, disconnected from the structured programmatic and impact data that would validate it. At the same time, datasets that illuminate the risks and opportunities facing a producer's business exist (rainfall, water stress, commodity prices, migration) but rarely reach producers, and producers rarely have the means to interpret them. FTUSA cannot yet systematically synthesize this multimodal evidence into decision-grade, verifiable insights fast enough for the AI-driven sourcing and compliance decisions that are increasingly shaping global commerce, while the 1.2 million+ producers across 52 countries who generate this evidence rarely see it come back to them as timely, data-backed guidance they can act on.

On Fair Trade USA's side, a Community Development Fund (Premium) investment, funds generated by Fair Trade Certified™ purchases for community-led investments, in water infrastructure is just a line item without context that it was a direct response to a water shortage the producer described. That stated context is what is often missing from impact reporting.

### Business Drivers

- Producer invisibility in AI-mediated commerce. As AI-powered search, recommendation engines, and commerce assistants become more influential, producers risk becoming invisible within the systems that shape market access, product discovery, and investment. The data standards and signals for that world are being established now.

- Regulatory pressure on commercial partners. Fair Trade brand and retail partners face growing requirements to substantiate sourcing and compliance (EUDR, CSRD, CSDDD, UFLPA, California Transparency in Supply Chains Act) and lack trusted producer-level outcome data to do so.

- Shift from allocation reporting to outcome reporting. Pairing producer-stated challenges with Premium project data and public climate evidence turns Community Development Fund allocations into documented evidence of resilience built.

- Strategic commitment. The project is the foundational infrastructure behind FTUSA's 2030 strategy (\$120M in annual Community Development Fund generation, 1.4 million producers and workers participating) and is governed as core infrastructure, not a pilot.

- Near-term timeline. Co-design and testing workshops with producer leaders in Peru, Honduras, and Côte d'Ivoire are planned for Q1 2027, with a public launch and live demonstration at World of Coffee in April 2027. A working exchange-agent slice from the hackathon feeds directly into those workshops.

### Business Constraints

- Producer consent and ownership are non-negotiable. Producers participate voluntarily, retain ownership of their data, and consent to outward sharing under Free, Prior, and Informed Consent protocols for Indigenous communities. A producer declining is a recorded, successful outcome, not an error.

- PII is flagged for human review rather than auto-redacted, because preserving authentic producer voice matters as much as protecting it.

- Every agent output is traceable: recommendations and insights must be grounded in FTUSA certification standards (Agricultural Production Standard and related standards) so they are not speculative, and every triage decision is logged with its reasoning.

- Scope stays bounded per agent. Each agent has a fixed toolset and a human gate, and the scope rule is enforced in the tool, not the prompt (for example, a data tool cannot return another producer's site). Adding a workload adds an agent rather than widening one.

- Agent-sourced records must be marked by source as "agent conversation" so they are never confused with mandatory or compliance reporting data.

- Hackathon constraints: two-day build; empty AWS sandbox with no access to FTUSA's environment or live systems; all data synthetic, anonymized samples provided by FTUSA, or publicly available open data.

### Success Metrics

The workshop is a two-day build in a sandbox with synthetic data. No producers are present; "producers" in every scenario are FTUSA and AWS testers role-playing from synthetic producer records. Nothing is deployed to real producers or producer organizations. Workshop success is therefore measured only by what can be observed and tested in the room: a working exchange loop, its correctness, its grounding, and its readiness for FTUSA to take forward.

## MVP Scope

### MVP Objective

Prove that an agent can take a producer's open-ended description of a challenge, confirm its understanding, return guidance grounded in the producer's own record, public data, and certification standards, and write a tagged, source-marked summary back into a longitudinal store, all within enforced scope and with a human review flag. The Document Analyst and Project Analyst agents, multimodal ingestion, and proactive climate-triggered outreach are out of scope.

### MVP Success Criteria

- \[ \] A tester playing a producer can complete one conversation end to end through a web chat interface, in English and in one additional language.

- \[ \] The agent opens with an open question about what has been difficult, follows up on timing, severity, and what has been tried, and confirms location and entity type before responding.

- \[ \] Before generating its response, the agent plays back its understanding and lets the producer correct it; the corrected version is what gets recorded.

- \[ \] The response contains (a) an insight about what the data shows for that challenge, crop, and region, and (b) at least one example of what a comparable producer did, each with a citation to its source.

- \[ \] Every insight and example is checked against the provided certification standards text and the response states which standard clause(s) it relates to.

- \[ \] The conversation is converted to a summary plus challenge tags from the sample tag set, linked to producer ID and entity type, marked source = "agent conversation", and written to the metadata store alongside the synthetic Premium, survey, and certification data.

- \[ \] The write-back record includes the content of the insight and examples given, not just the producer's stated challenge.

- \[ \] At the end of the conversation, the agent asks whether the guidance was helpful and records the response.

- \[ \] A PII flag is raised for human review when the producer names an individual or shares contact details; nothing is auto-redacted.

- \[ \] A producer who declines to continue or declines to have the record stored produces a recorded "declined" outcome, not an error.

- \[ \] Negative tests prove the data tools cannot return another producer's data.

### MVP User Journeys

#### Journey 1: Producer describes a challenge and receives grounded guidance

1.  A tester, acting as a producer from the synthetic dataset (e.g., a coffee smallholder in Cauca, Colombia), opens the web chat and is greeted with an open question about what has been difficult recently.

2.  The producer describes the challenge in their own words (e.g., rainfall has been unreliable for two seasons and the last harvest was down).

3.  The agent follows up: when it started, how severe, what has already been tried, and confirms the producer's location and entity type from the session record.

4.  The agent plays back its understanding; the producer corrects one detail (e.g., it is a cooperative, not an individual farm).

5.  The agent retrieves this producer's own Premium and survey history, regional open data matched to location and crop, and comparable Premium projects from peers, checking each against the certification standards.

6.  The agent returns an insight ("CHIRPS data for your region shows rainfall X% below the 10-year average over the last two seasons; Aqueduct projects increasing water stress") and peer examples ("Two cooperatives in similar conditions funded rainwater catchment and shade-tree planting under their Premium plan"), each with a citation and related standard clause.

7.  The agent asks whether it may store a summary of this conversation in the producer's record; the producer agrees.**Outcome**: The producer leaves with evidence and precedent they otherwise would not have had, framed to inform their own decision rather than direct it.**Limitation vs Full Vision**: Text only, web only, two languages, pre-loaded open data for a handful of regions, synthetic producer records, no live connection to FTUSA systems, and no proactive outreach.

#### Journey 2: Producer declines to participate

8.  The producer begins describing a challenge, then says they would rather not continue or do not want this stored.

9.  The agent acknowledges, does not retrieve or store conversation content, and records a "declined" outcome against the producer ID with a timestamp and no narrative content.**Outcome**: A recorded, successful close with consent respected.**Limitation vs Full Vision**: Consent UX for Indigenous FPIC protocols is simplified to a yes/no in the MVP **\[INFERRED\]**.

#### Journey 3: FTUSA reviewer sees the conversation become a reusable record

10. After the exchange, a tester acting as an Impact, Research & Learning reviewer opens the minimal review view.

11. They see the new record: producer ID, entity type, language, summary of the challenge in the producer's words, challenge tags (e.g., "climate and weather", "water"), source = "agent conversation", review status = pending, PII flag if raised, and the agent's reasoning log.

12. The reviewer approves or corrects the tags and summary; the record is marked reviewed.

13. They query the metadata store and see the record joined to the same producer's synthetic Premium projects, survey responses, and certification record.**Outcome**: FTUSA holds a producer-stated account of what a Premium investment was responding to, joinable with the quantitative data.**Limitation vs Full Vision**: Single-record review in a minimal view; no queue management, no sync into Atlas/Redshift, no region-level aggregation.

#### Journey 4: Producer asks something outside the agent's scope

14. A producer asks the agent for an answer or action it is not designed to handle, such as checking their certification status or raising a worker voice complaint.

15. The agent recognizes the request falls outside what it is built to handle, declines to answer or act on it, and tells the producer why.

16. The conversation ends there, or returns to the open question if the producer also has an actual challenge to describe; nothing from the misdirected request is written to the record.**Outcome**: The producer gets a clear decline instead of an improvised or absorbed response.

### MVP Constraints and Assumptions

- **Assumption**: FTUSA provides the anonymized sample dataset (Premium projects and survey results with location and entity type) and the APS and FPS text as machine-readable, clause-numbered Excel with PDF backup before the hackathon. **Status:** Confirmed; sample datasets and related notes are in the shared Box folder.

- **Assumption**: A handful of open datasets (CHIRPS, Aqueduct, World Bank commodity prices at minimum) can be pre-extracted for the pilot regions and loaded as static tables; live API or Earth Engine calls are not needed. **Risk if wrong**: Regional-context tool slips; fallback is a hand-curated regional fact table clearly labeled as such.

- **Assumption**: A single agent with five or six narrow tools is sufficient; no multi-agent orchestration is needed for this slice. **Risk if wrong**: Response quality suffers; mitigation is better tool design, not more agents.

- **Assumption**: Claude on Amazon Bedrock handles Spanish-language conversation well enough that Amazon Translate is not required in the loop. **Risk if wrong**: Add Translate at the input/output boundary.

- **Accepted Limitation**: Synthetic and anonymized data only; no real producer is represented and nothing is deployed to producers.

- **Accepted Limitation**: The stand-in metadata store mirrors FTUSA's DynamoDB-plus-Redshift pattern in shape but is not connected to Atlas; the join is demonstrated within the sandbox.

- **Accepted Limitation**: Evaluation uses a small labeled set created during the workshop, not FTUSA's existing benchmark corpus.

### Candidate Third-Party Datasets (Illustrative, Non-Prescriptive)

Ideas and thought starters, not requirements. Focused on nature-related data first: climate, water, soil and land, and biodiversity.

- **Climate and weather**: CHIRPS (rainfall), ERA5 (temperature/precipitation reanalysis) - flags shifting rainfall or heat patterns and supports forward-looking statements.

- **Soil and land**: SoilGrids (soil carbon, pH, degradation), JRC Tropical Moist Forest (forest cover/loss) - flags erosion or degradation risk for agroforestry or soil-health projects.

- **Water**: Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas - projects future water stress, not just current conditions.

- **Biodiversity and land tenure**: WDPA (protected areas), LandMark (indigenous and community lands) - relevant to stewardship claims and FPIC-related context.
